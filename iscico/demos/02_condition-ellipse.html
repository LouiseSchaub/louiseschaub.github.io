Condition Number Ellipse

A 2 × 2 matrix A maps the unit circle onto an ellipse. Its longest half-axis is σ₁ = ‖A‖₂ and its shortest is σ₂ = 1/‖A⁻¹‖₂, so cond₂(A) = σ₁/σ₂ says how flat the ellipse is. Change the matrix and compare the half-axes with the eigenvalues.

How to read the picture

Half-axes are singular values. The ellipse is the set of all A x with ‖x‖₂ = 1. Its longest half-axis has length σ₁ = max ‖A x‖₂ = ‖A‖₂. Its shortest has length σ₂ = min ‖A x‖₂ = 1/‖A⁻¹‖₂. Hence cond₂(A) = σ₁/σ₂ is the ratio of the two half-axes: a round ellipse means a well-conditioned matrix, a flat one an ill-conditioned matrix. σ₁² and σ₂² are the eigenvalues of AᵀA (corrected Lemma 2.3).

Symmetric A: eigenvectors lie on the axes. The eigenvectors of a symmetric matrix are perpendicular, and A only stretches them by λ. So they point along the half-axes, and σᵢ = |λᵢ|. For a symmetric positive definite matrix all λ are positive and cond₂(A) = λ_max/λ_min, as printed in the notes. With a negative eigenvalue the arrow λ v points against v, and only the absolute values give cond₂.

Non-symmetric A: eigenvectors and axes separate. Try the shear: both eigenvalues are 1, so the eigenvalue formula gives 1, but the ellipse is clearly flat and cond₂ = 4. With the rotation and scaling there are no real eigen-directions at all, while the ellipse is a circle and cond₂ = 1.
