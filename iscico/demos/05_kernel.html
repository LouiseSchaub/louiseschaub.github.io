Kernel Interpolation and Its Width

The kernel-based model s(x) = Σ αᵢ k(x, xᵢ) puts a Gaussian k(x, xᵢ) = exp(−‖x − xᵢ‖²/σ²) on every data point and solves Aα = y with the Cholesky decomposition (Theorem 5.2, Algorithm 6). Everything depends on the width σ: too small and s collapses between the points, too large and A becomes nearly singular.

What to try

Small σ. Each Gaussian is a narrow spike. A is almost the identity, α ≈ y, and s drops to 0 between the points: it interpolates, but it does not approximate.

Large σ. All Gaussians look alike on the scale of the data, the entries of A all approach 1, and A becomes nearly singular. cond(A) explodes, the coefficients αᵢ become huge with alternating signs, and the terms cancel. With many points the interpolant starts to overshoot, like a polynomial of high degree. This is what happens in Exercise 3c with σ = 1.

In between σ should be comparable to the distance between neighbouring points and to the scale on which f changes.
