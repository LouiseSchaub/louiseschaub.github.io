Quadrature on Triangles

Every triangle T is the image of the reference triangle T̂ under an affine map Φ. The transformation formula turns the vertex rule on T̂, ⅙ (f(0, 0) + f(1, 0) + f(0, 1)), into the rule |T|/3 · (f(P₁) + f(P₂) + f(P₃)) on T. Drag the vertices, choose a function, and refine.

What to try

The determinant. |det DΦ| is always twice the area |T|, because T̂ has area ½. Forgetting the ½ doubles every result. Drag one vertex onto the line through the other two: det DΦ → 0, the triangle degenerates, and Φ is no longer invertible.

Exactness. For the linear function the vertex rule is exact on every triangle (Exercise 2a and 2b): f ∘ Φ is linear again. For x² it is not: the rule integrates the linear interpolant.

Refinement. For smooth f each refinement divides the error by about 4: order 2 in h, the same as the composite trapezoidal rule in 1D. Exercise 4a(ii) is exactly this, on a grid of squares split into two triangles each.
