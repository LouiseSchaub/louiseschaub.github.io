From Interpolation to Quadrature

What to try

Trapezoidal rule, n = 1. The coloured region between f and the chord is the error. For a convex function (eˣ, x², x⁴, (x + 1)⁻¹, √(1 + x²)) the chord lies above the graph and the rule overestimates; for sin x, concave on [0, 2], it underestimates.

Simpson's rule, n = 2. The error regions now have both signs and partly cancel. The weights 1/3, 4/3, 1/3 (in units of h) are the integrals of the three basis polynomials; switch on the terms f(xᵢ)Lᵢ to see them. They always add up to b − a = 2.

Exactness. The trapezoidal rule is exact up to degree 1, Simpson's rule up to degree 3, one more than its interpolant: this is Exercise 2b. The same happens for n = 4. For n = 3 there is no bonus.
