The Rod in Ice Water

One way to read Exercise 2: a thin metal rod of length l has the same temperature u₀ everywhere. At t = 0 both ends are dipped into ice water and held at temperature 0. How does the rod cool down? This is an interpretation; the sheet only states the equations.

The curve is the series solution u(t, x) = Σ Aₙ sin(nπx/l) e^(−k(nπ/l)² t) from the sheet, plotted as u/u₀ over x/l against the dimensionless time τ = kt/l². The bar below the plot is the rod, coloured by temperature.

The readoutThe centre stays close to u₀ at first, then cools. Since τ = kt/l², a rod twice as long needs four times as long to reach the same state.
Boundary: NeumannNothing happens: the rod stays at u₀. With insulated ends no heat leaves, and the rod is already uniform.
